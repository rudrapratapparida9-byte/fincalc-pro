Smart Financial Planning: The Power of Compounding & Early Debt Payoff

Achieving financial independence requires understanding two fundamental financial forces: compound growth on your investments and amortization interest drag on your liabilities.

1. How Extra Mortgage Payments Save Tens of Thousands in Interest

In standard 30-year fixed loans, the majority of your early monthly payments go directly toward interest rather than principal reduction. By adding just an extra $100 to $200 each month toward the principal, you can shave 4 to 7 years off your total payoff timeline and save up to 30% in cumulative interest.

2. The Rule of 72 and Wealth Compounding (SIP)

The Rule of 72 is a quick formula to estimate how many years it takes for your investment to double:

Years to Double = 72 ÷ Annual Rate of Return (%)

At a 12% annual return rate in index equity funds, your wealth doubles roughly every 6 years. Starting 5 years earlier can result in more than double the final retirement corpus.
